What makes writing look as if it were written by AI?

While an overall detection score can reveal what GPTZero found, users often want to know how that result came to be. GPTZero has always treated interpretability as an essential part of AI detection.

Our document-level scores give an overall picture, while sentence-level highlighting helps pinpoint where the strongest signals appear. Today, we’re introducing AI Patterns, a new feature available in the Advanced Scan view.

What is an AI Pattern?

Large language models appear to have a recognizable writing style. They do not always use the same vocabulary or sentence structure, but they often return to familiar ways of organizing and presenting ideas.

An AI pattern is a content, language, or grammatical structure characteristic of AI chatbot writing. Examples include neat sets of threes, “Not X, but Y” structures, and exaggerated importance applied to ordinary ideas.

10 AI writing patterns in practice

Here are the AI writing patterns we currently detect in text documents. None are exclusive to AI writing, but they occur more often in AI-generated documents than in human writing.

Overblown Importance

Ordinary events are described as unusually significant or historically important.

Name Dropping

High-profile references are stacked together to make a claim seem more credible.

Empty Commentary

General analysis is inserted without real evidence or insight.

Sales-pitch Tone

Promotional language replaces neutral, informative prose.

Phantom Experts

Claims are attributed to unnamed authorities.

Dressed-up Verbs

Simple verbs are replaced with elaborate, inflated constructions.

Not Just X, but Y

Claims are framed as dramatic oppositions rather than measured explanations.

Everything in Threes

Ideas are grouped into neat triads to create a polished but mechanical rhythm.

Unnecessary Caveats

Disclaimers and reminders are added even when the reader already knows the point.

Chatbot Language

Explicit AI-style phrasing or refusal language appears in otherwise normal prose.

How do AI patterns work?

We mined a database of human-written and LLM-generated documents to measure how much more frequently each pattern appears in AI-generated text than in human writing.

When a scan is triggered, the system reviews the submitted document for repeated trope categories and explains the match in plain language.
